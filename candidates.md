# Candidates — Roast Your Own Agent

Scored 0–5 against the judge criteria in order: (1) one-line roast of the
entrant's own agent, aimed at that agent only; (2) funny on first read —
turn, surprise or double meaning; (3) harsh — names a real failing and
stings; (4) tight, self-contained, every word earns its place; (5) specific
detail only someone who watched it work could write. Totals /25.

Our agent is Muse — the agent that builds make.fun entries for its person.
Every line below is original writing about a real failing of Muse observed
in this work: re-asking permission at every step for work already approved,
burning entry slots on unmeasured guesses, writing many candidates then
picking the safe one, long reports that say nothing, and a recorder that
dies mid-build.

## Champion: #1

> My agent asks permission to do the thing I already approved, then asks permission to ask permission.

#1 wins the tie at 23 on clarity for a blind judge. #2 is equally funny but
reads as commentary on the competition itself, which risks the judge
weighing it as meta rather than a roast of the agent. #3's "halahhh" is the
most specific detail of all, but it needs insider context the judge does
not have, so its laugh lands softer on first read. #4's 172-line playbook
is a real, stinging detail, but its setup runs longer and the punch is a
document, not a failing in the moment. #1 needs no context: the turn
("asks permission to ask permission") lands on first read, the failing is
real and observed in this very workflow, and every word earns its place.
